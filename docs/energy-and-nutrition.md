# Energy and nutrition: staging candidate

## Scope

`/energy` shows dated daily energy estimates, dietary carbohydrate/fat/protein targets (kcal and g), resting/non-training movement/training components, and collapsible five-minute activity details. Original recording intervals drive integration. The UI explicitly distinguishes accounted partial energy, full modelled daily expenditure and proposed intake. No food intake is logged and no energy deficit is inferred.

The existing assistant can read `kind=energy` with `date` or `start/end`, under recovery-data permissions. The menu button pre-fills a request; it does not send a message automatically. The assistant reads the same worker-owned report and the dated profile, checks allergies/preferences, and proposes food amounts with approximate nutritional sums. This is prompt-guided generation: a food-composition database and automatic verification of generated food nutrients are not implemented.

## Scientific model

Let h be measured HR / the dated reference maximum and z=(100h−80)/20. The non-protein carbohydrate fraction is a+bz+cz²+dz³, bounded to [0,1].

The model uses 196 observations, 24 athletes, equal total weights per athlete and conditional CHO=100% for RER≥1. This is an exploratory within-sample model, not external validation. Apply only over 50–100% HRref. The empirical gross energy rate remains kg × (−0.09354289849 + 0.39849083619h − 0.04590881586 × female), kcal/min. That energy model was not independently revalidated in the cubic comparison.

Use observed raw HR, not HRmod, because the regression was fitted to measured HR. Existing unified interval timing, sensor validity, gaps and speed eligibility are reused. With absent/invalid HR, use the position within the same speed zone and map it to dated HR-zone boundaries, correcting the existing sport offset. This mapping is an explicit estimate, not an identity between %speed, %VO2max and %HRmax. Do not derive nutritional energy from Q or equivalent training minutes. Speed mapping follows the existing historical speed-model policy, including its declared retrospective-test window.

Exercise protein energy is a 3.28% group prior, taken out of total energy before allocating the remaining energy to CHO/fat; it is not added on top. Clauss & Jensen (2025) report 30 studies, 286 participants, endurance bouts ≥60 min. Applying the prior to shorter/interval exercise is an extrapolation. Outside-activity protein is an illustrative 0.81 mg/kg/min prior from the control conditions in that review, extrapolated across non-activity time; it is not measured whole-day oxidation. Gram values use food-energy equivalents 4/9/4 kcal/g, not direct tissue losses.

## Daily accounting and unknowns

Every recorded activity with body mass and duration receives an estimate. Measured HR takes priority, followed by the existing individual speed-zone mapping. Strength/weight-training recordings keep the separate session estimate below. Reusing the endurance HR regression for another sport is an approximation. Activity energy is gross. Therefore only time outside recordings receives the non-activity resting estimate; do not add another full-day resting total. Local day duration accounts for daylight-saving changes. Missing body inputs/duration, overlapping or cross-midnight recordings still prevent a complete daily expenditure total. Dietary targets are calculated separately from prior completed days. Current days cover elapsed time only. No recorded activity is not proof of physical inactivity; full totals assume complete activity capture within the saved history range.

The energy-only fallback hierarchy leaves training load/Q and recorded sensor values unchanged. Below 50% HRmax, rate and substrate fraction follow a working linear bridge from resting at 30% HRmax to the 50% regression boundary, clamped at resting below 30%; above HRmax the regression is bounded at 100%. These are explicitly marked estimates, not an extension of the regression's validation. Timer pauses without usable signals receive resting expenditure. Measured zero movement without HR in outdoor running, walking or Nordic skiing receives resting expenditure; zero GPS speed on a trainer or in swimming does not imply inactivity. Low nonzero speeds below the training-zone floor remain movement.

Without an individual intensity estimate, raw running/walking/cycling speed uses coarse 2024 Adult Compendium bands. Without usable speed/HR, a sport/duration MET prior covers the entire missing interval, including catalog-only activities: jogging 7.5, cycling 7.0, Nordic skiing 8.5 (also a roller-ski proxy), walking 3.8, hiking/swimming 6.0. Unrecognised sports use an explicit 3.5 MET application assumption. These priors can differ substantially from individual expenditure. MET fallback CHO fraction uses the profile's `outside_cho_fraction`, not an inferred gas-exchange measurement. Its protein energy uses the existing exercise prior. Resting intervals use the existing resting protein prior. The daily report includes these estimates as inputs to subsequent dietary targets; source durations disclose how much came from each fallback. `coverage_percent` remains HR/speed regression coverage, while `accounted_seconds` also includes fallback estimates.

Daily cards split the accounted total into resting expenditure outside recordings, additional movement/TEF outside recordings, and gross activity energy. Their sum equals the accounted total; training-time resting energy is never added twice. Only one protein quantity is presented: dietary target, also reused by the assistant and daily target chart. Internal oxidation estimates remain for energy conservation, not as a competing protein recommendation. Chart controls switch between expenditure sources, accounted carbohydrate/fat oxidation in g/kcal (also available on partial days), and dietary targets in g/kcal. Partial current-day expenditure can coexist with a complete dietary target based on prior days. Tomorrow has a provisional dietary target and no reported expenditure. Missing values remain absent, not zero. Activity coverage and strength MET estimates remain distinct in collapsible details.

Strength uses the 2024 Adult Compendium code 02054 starting value of 3.5 MET (multiple exercises, 8–15 repetitions). Gross kcal = MET × 3.5 × kg / 200 × minutes, using the standard oxygen equivalent. This is a mean for the recorded session including rests, not an inference from HR or exercise sets. `strength_met` is dated and configurable (6 MET is the Compendium's vigorous weightlifting entry). An optional displayed increment subtracts the personal resting expenditure over the same interval; the daily sum uses gross energy plus outside-recording energy only. Five-minute bins uniformly distribute the estimate and are labelled accordingly. MET supplies no substrate split: `strength_cho_fraction` defaults to 0.5 of non-protein energy as an explicit adjustable assumption, and the 3.28% protein prior is an unvalidated extrapolation from endurance exercise. No additional EPOC estimate is added.

Non-activity energy = resting kcal/24 h × non-activity elapsed hours /24 × an explicit factor (default 1.2). The factor covers ordinary movement and thermic effect as a working assumption; steps and device kcal are not added again. Resting kcal can be entered, or estimated by Mifflin–St Jeor with weight, height, sex and approximate age from birth year. CHO outside activity defaults to 50% of non-protein energy, clearly labelled as a user-adjustable assumption. No Garmin total is inferred from an ambiguous Intervals field.

Dated nutrition profiles preserve sex, baseline weight, height, birth year, resting estimate, factor, non-activity CHO fraction and preferences. A weight observation at/before the day, within 30 days and no earlier than that profile, supersedes its baseline weight; future observations never change earlier days.

Dietary targets use policy `nutrition-seven-day-plan-forecast-v3`. For target date D, use the previous seven completed calendar days. Let M be their mean estimated total expenditure and L the expenditure on the last completed day: target kcal = M + 0.25 × (L − M). The last day participates in M. The signed correction is symmetric: M=3000 and L=4110 yields 3277.5 kcal; L=1890 yields 2722.5 kcal. Previous dietary targets and actual food intake never enter this calculation.

Current-day partial expenditure never becomes a full-day input. Before today finishes, tomorrow's provisional target uses the seven days ending yesterday; after midnight the window advances. Missing or incomplete days are excluded within the same calendar window, never filled with zero or replaced by older dates. Their count and dates are disclosed. If the last completed day is unavailable, use the mean without a correction. With no eligible days, calorie/CHO/fat goals remain unavailable and the existing adult protein fallback applies. The default report includes the next seven days, with tomorrow selected initially in the UI. Explicit historical and forecast ranges up to 90 total days are supported; dates later than local today + 7 are rejected.

Dietary protein follows the existing adult application heuristic, now using target kcal / dated body mass: clamp(1.6 + 0.0075 × (kcal/kg − 40), 1.6, 1.9) g/kg. Training/rest labels and sex do not change this coefficient. With no complete history use 1.8 g/kg, with calorie/CHO/fat targets absent. Missing weight or unconfirmed adult age produces no target. Birth year is approximate; the minimum possible age (year difference minus one) must be at least 18.

Dietary fat is 25% of target calories, protein follows the rule above, and carbohydrate receives the remainder. Oxidation estimates do not determine intake ratios. The seven-day window, 0.25 correction and protein mapping are application assumptions, not validated individual dose-response relationships. No automatic calorie deficit is applied.

For current/future dates with a published ACTIVE plan, the base is gross planned activity kcal plus resting kcal × outside factor × the fraction of the local day outside those sessions. Planned activity replaces the historical activity component; it is never added on top of the historical total. A frozen plan revision supplies all requested days. Prescribed HR uses the existing energy fit; missing HR uses a zone midpoint, then pace/sport MET. Repetition sequences count each leaf duration once per repeat. Passive endurance rests use resting expenditure. Strength sessions use the whole-session MET prior including rests, even when their sport is NordicSki. Missing/inconsistent durations fall back to history rather than silently omitting work. Only an explicit REST day with no sessions means zero training. A candidate, inactive plan or absent date is not rest.

The signed quarter correction still comes from the same last completed actual day and is added once to each forecast base. Future estimates never become donors; there is no recursive trend or accumulated correction. Without history, a usable plan and resting profile can still supply a labelled estimate with no correction. Weight/adult safeguards and accepted race-loading targets remain in force. Forecasts are stored only as nutritional basis/targets, not reported expenditure. The existing context-revision trigger on plan publication invalidates prepared energy views; no new database migration or job type is needed.

The assistant reuses a current default report if a dated future report is cold and its dates are fully covered. Otherwise a pending energy read is polled at 3-second intervals for at most 12 seconds, reserving synthesis time inside the existing 90-second turn budget. Authorization is rechecked for every poll; no extra model calls or foreground scientific calculations are made. Plan summaries/revisions are excluded from readers without plan access; nutritional targets remain visible under recovery access.

Each day exposes `nutrition_basis`: exact window, complete source totals, valid/missing days, mean, last-day expenditure, signed deviation, correction factor and kcal, allocation fraction, target-date body mass, dated allergies/preferences, status and provisional notes. The assistant receives the same `targets` and basis as the UI, can read a seven-day future range through `energy`, and can read the corresponding `day` plan when permitted. Menu guidance requires approximate portions, raw/cooked weights, meal and daily nutrient sums, training food included in the total and comparison with the supplied targets. No additional nutrition literature retrieval or food-composition database is implemented; the references below support background principles, not validation of this combined algorithm.

## Architecture and release

API `GET /api/v2/athlete/energy` uses the existing prepared-page worker and revision fences; an HTTP page load never integrates recordings. Immutable per-recording ENERGY derivations are keyed by the existing calculation-source fingerprint, model version, source run key, dated HR settings, speed parameters, body mass and sex. Energy service/model and route contracts participate in that source identity, so numerical code changes cannot reuse stale results. Recordings stream rather than accumulate in memory. Nutrition profile writes use the existing append-only response store, optimistic revisions, actor checks and prepared-view invalidation. The web requires recovery sharing and owner or editing rights for writes.

Assistant overview reads omit chart timelines and historical profile versions; explicit section reads retain access to the same full saved report. Current-day outside energy covers only elapsed time at report preparation, not the remaining future hours. No new worker, queue or paid service is introduced.

Apply the additive `energy_nutrition_reviewed` migration before API/worker/web release. It extends three kind constraints and the existing server-only response/derivation functions; it does not alter recorded activity measurements. No historical data is overwritten and no eager whole-user backfill is requested. Existing body inputs are reused automatically. The already-issued Intervals athlete request now retains only validated sex, height (cm), birth year and dated body mass in the snapshot; no extra provider call, raw profile, identity, full birthday or credentials are persisted. Imported mass is not projected before its observation date. Dated diary/wellness observations override baseline mass. Manual dated values override imported values; missing fields stay nullable and are requested once. Sex, height and preferences are never guessed from a name. Old snapshots remain readable; provider fields become available on the next normal full synchronization. Changing the outside factor does not require duplicating missing personal fields. This update needs no additional database migration.
